03·Case study · AMA Holdings · The Loaded Teashop

The growth desk behind US$44M of creator ads.

AMA Holdings is the US company that owns The Loaded Teashop and Goli Nutrition. The Loaded Teashop sells direct to consumer through a creator ad program: hundreds of creators uploading UGC to briefs, media buyers scaling the winners on Meta and TikTok. Since October 2026 I've been the program's growth and automation specialist. I keep the whole team looking at the same numbers, and I make the busywork between a creator's upload and a live ad run by itself.

Every figure on this page comes from the program's own systems (the creator platform, Meta, Triple Whale and Northbeam) as of 2 November 2026. Team and client scale: the media buyers spend the money, the creators make the videos, I build the loop that connects them.

The loop

From upload to live ad, and the numbers back.

Creators upload. The team approves. Media buyers launch. The results decide the next brief. Before, each hand-off was a person copying files and rows. Now it looks like this.

  • Every minute

    New uploads land in the QA queue with flavor, funnel stage and whitelist handles filled in, and anything missing flagged on the card.

  • Every 15 minutes

    Approved videos go to Drive and the creative sheet, and the ad feed refreshes spend, ROAS and pass or fail for every video.

  • Every hour, every day, every week

    Creators sync, upload history and the submissions log run on the hour. Reports post to Telegram three times a day. The top 50 gets read every week.

What I run

Three jobs, one person.

Growth specialist is the title. In practice it splits into reading the data, running the creators and building the systems, and the point is that the same person does all three.

Growth

Read what wins, every week

The 50 videos with the most spend among the week's uploads, each downloaded, transcribed and sampled frame by frame, then sorted by angle, hook, buildup, body, CTA and format.

  • Median thumbstop, ROAS and CPA per family of winners, so a brief can ask for the family, not a guess.
  • Audits of creator videos against those findings, written so a creator can act on them.
  • One rule for a winner, applied to every video by the dashboard: ROAS 1.4× and CPA $26 or better on $100+ of spend.
  • Brief, product and funnel performance tables the team can quote in the Monday call.
Creators

833 creators, one database

Handles, whitelist status, tier and drop lists in one table the whole team edits, instead of five spreadsheets that disagreed.

  • Hourly sync from the creator platform fills missing handles and marks who is set up. Team edits are never overwritten.
  • A video whose creator is not whitelisted is held with the reason on the card, so nothing ships that can't run.
  • Creator Scout: a chat command that searches TikTok and Instagram, scores each creator against the ideal profile and returns a shortlist.
  • Roster reads: who is uploading, who went quiet, who got samples and never posted.
Automation

20+ workflows that report themselves

n8n on top of Supabase, Vercel and the Claude API. Every scheduled job writes a run line, and a failure turns a card red on the dashboard instead of going quiet.

  • The video pipeline: upload to media buyers in 15 minutes, with QA, whitelist checks and daily reports.
  • Ad feeds: spend and ROAS from Meta, Triple Whale and Northbeam, refreshed every 15 minutes.
  • A knowledge bot on web and Telegram that answers from 900+ team notes and cites them.
  • An AI editing app that turns creators' phone clips into finished 9:16 ads.

Systems

Every 15 minutes the approved videos are downloaded, filed in Drive by day, flavor, creator and funnel stage, and written to the media buyers' creative sheet with the right ad copy. Winners, plainly

What counts as a winning video in a creator ad program?

The team's rule, and the one the dashboard applies to every video: at least $100 of spend, a blended ROAS of 1.4× or better and a cost per purchase of $26 or less. In the 30 days to 2 November 2026, 2,013 videos were judged and 239 passed, about 12%. That ratio is normal. A few videos carry most of the results, which is why the weekly analysis exists: find what the 239 have in common and brief more of it.

What does a growth specialist do in a creator ad program?

Keep everyone looking at the same numbers and shorten the loop between them. In practice: a weekly read of the top 50 videos by spend (angle, hook, CTA and format, with ROAS and thumbstop per family), audits of creator videos against those findings, a creator database the whole team edits, and the automation that moves an approved video to the media buyers in 15 minutes instead of a day. Media buyers spend the money and creators make the videos. I make sure the data from one reaches the other.
